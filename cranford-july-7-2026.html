CRANFORD, NEW JERSEY · JULY 7, 2026

The council meeting that went national

On the evening of Tuesday, July 7, 2026, a resident in a giant purple bong costume stepped up to the microphone during the public-comment portion of the Cranford Township Committee meeting, spelled his name for the record — B-O-N-G-H-O-L-E-O — and performed a parody of Lizzo's "About Damn Time." Within forty-eight hours the clip was on TMZ, and within a week it had run on USA Today, Stereogum, News 12 New Jersey and NJ 101.5, and aired on Jimmy Kimmel Live.

What actually happened

Cranford's committee meetings, like every New Jersey municipal meeting, set aside time for residents to address the governing body. Bongholeo used his. He introduced himself in character, spelled the name out so the clerk could take it down, introduced the baby-bong prop, and then performed — a rewritten version of a pop song, in costume, at a public microphone, on the township's own meeting recording.

The joke is the delivery. The argument underneath it is not a joke: public comment is the one part of a municipal meeting that belongs to residents, and a town's instinct to shut it down — through time limits, prop bans, or a mayor's hand on the microphone — is the thing worth watching. That is what he has been documenting across New Jersey for years, and it is why the tape is on the record rather than only on a phone.

Local government exists to serve the people who live in the community. The costume is absurd. The principle is not.

The arrest at that meeting was not Bongholeo

This is the single most common thing people get wrong about July 7. Later in the same Cranford meeting, a different speaker — William Thilly — was removed and arrested after his own public comment, which ended with him singing Morrissey. News reports at the time said police charged him with aggravated assault on a law-enforcement officer, among other counts.

Those charges belong to that speaker, not to Bongholeo, and they are allegations that had not been adjudicated when they were reported. Bongholeo finished his comment and left the podium. Coverage that describes "the bong guy" being arrested is conflating two different people at one meeting.

What happened next

Cranford was not a one-off and it was not the beginning. Through July and August 2026 the costume turned up in Kenilworth over a $1.8 billion AI data center, in Roselle Park, in Jackson, in Parsippany, and in Franklin Township in Somerset County, where residents fought the township's Flock Safety automated licence-plate-reader contract — a meeting NJ.com and New Jersey 101.5 both covered, naming him in their reporting.
